Phase 1: Revenue Mix Simulator
Cremica operates in two channels: B2C (branded retail products sold to consumers) and B2B (supplying biscuits and sauces to Quick Service Restaurants like McDonald’s). B2C drives higher revenue growth potential through brand building and wider distribution, but requires heavy marketing spend. B2B offers more stable, higher margins with lower customer acquisition costs due to long-term contracts. Here you decide the strategic focus — growth via retail or stability via institutional business.

Phase 2: Cost Pressure Scenarios
FMCG companies like Cremica face frequent raw material and logistics inflation. When input costs rise, management must decide how to respond: absorb the hit (protect volume/market share), pass it on via price hikes (risk volume), or offset through internal efficiency. You will face three real-world cost shocks and choose the best response to protect EBITDA margins.

Phase 3: Unit Economics Lens
Not all revenue is equal. Here you compare the fundamental economics of a single B2C retail pack versus a B2B QSR contract. B2C has higher average selling price per unit but requires significant marketing spend and longer payback periods. B2B has lower price per kg but near-zero customer acquisition cost and fast payback due to recurring volume and long contracts. The question: which business actually generates the cash that funds future growth and brand building?

Choose wisely — this decision defines the resilience of Cremica’s profitability engine.

Key Insight Summary

What drives margins

Stable B2B contracts and operational efficiency provide the foundation for healthy profitability.

What risks margins

Raw material volatility and heavy retail dependence can erode margins quickly.

Why Cremica remains resilient

Balanced B2C-B2B mix allows B2B cash flow to fund B2C brand building while protecting overall margins.
